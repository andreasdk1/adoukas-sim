Junction / design limit
    Flow
    2D incompressible flow over the board on a staggered grid, with pressure projection and friction from the channel walls. Tall parts (inductor, capacitors) are solid obstacles, so wakes and vortex shedding form behind them.
    Heat
    The board is a conducting sheet (copper plus FR4) with each part's power as a heat source. It loses heat to the air through a local convection coefficient that follows the local air speed (laminar flat-plate correlation), by radiation to the enclosure, and through its underside; the air carries its share downstream. Each low-profile package also sheds part of its power through its own top surface, by convection to the air and radiation to the enclosure. At the default settings about two thirds of the heat ends up in the air, a fifth leaves through the underside and the rest is radiated.
    Junctions
    Junction temperature = board temperature under the part + the power flowing into the board × junction-to-board thermal resistance. The split between the board and the package top follows from the junction-to-board and junction-to-case-top resistances, both typical datasheet values. Limits are conservative design limits, not absolute maximums: 110 °C for MOSFETs rated 150–175 °C, 85 °C for 105 °C-rated electrolytics (their life halves with every 10 °C), 85 °C for the MCU, 100 °C for the inductor.
    Limits
    A 2D, depth-averaged model on a 0.8 mm grid: right for trends and layout intuition, not for sign-off. A real design gets a full 3D conjugate heat-transfer model in Icepak or OpenFOAM.
